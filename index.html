Sourcing playbooks

🔎 — Turning a job description into LinkedIn and X-ray search strings

Start from the must-haves, not the title. I group synonyms into OR-blocks (titles, core stack, domain), join the blocks with AND, and cut the noise with NOT (recruiters, students, agencies). The same logic then becomes an X-ray string: site:linkedin.com/in plus quoted phrases.

Test each block on its own first. If one block returns nothing, the whole query will too.

🧭 — Three one-line directions before writing a single query

H1, H2, H3: one line each, naming the angle and what makes it different (stack, geography or title). It's a triage step: pick a direction in seconds, then expand only the one that looks promising.

🧪 — Where else the right people might be hiding

Five logics I rotate through: title substitution (the same job under another name), industry pivot (the same skills in a neighbouring domain), geographic arbitrage, stack-based inference (what else people who use X usually know) and career-transition patterns.

Each hypothesis gets its own titles, keywords and donor companies.

🗺️ — Cover the whole market instead of guessing the one best query

Split the addressable pool along a few independent axes (company profile, stack specialisation, one or two role-specific axes) so the segments barely overlap and together cover everything.

Then track net-new profiles per segment and stop when a segment stops producing them.

✉️ — Short, self-aware first messages for passive candidates

Personalisation comes from the profile, the hook comes from the role itself, and a little humour covers the fact that yes, this is another recruiter message. I write five variants per candidate and send the one that sounds most human.

📝 — Internal role cards turned into honest public job posts

Lead with the actual challenge, be straight about what's hard, cut the buzzwords and leave out anything confidential. A good post filters as much as it attracts.

Dashboards & AI in hiring

📊 — Outreach volume, reply rates and funnel conversion per person

ATS exports go in and a single interactive HTML dashboard comes out: who reached out to whom, which messages got replies, and how candidates moved through each stage. It turns the weekly "how are we doing?" into a five-minute read.

🧑‍💼 — Interview velocity, workload and stage conversion per recruiter

This shows who is overloaded before it turns into missed candidates, and where in the process people drop out and why.

🤖 — One precise brief an AI sourcing agent can actually run

The brief names the one axis that really separates strong candidates from the rest, flags where the agent is likely to misread the role, and limits the agent to search only. Before anything runs at scale, it asks for a sanity check on every axis.

🧮 — A transparent calculator that ties bonuses to results

Everyone can see exactly how their numbers turn into money. Quality of pipeline counts, not just volume.

📈 — Week-over-week trends per vacancy, turned into a short readout

What moved, what stalled and what to change next week, with one short paragraph per role.
